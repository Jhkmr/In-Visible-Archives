In Visible Archives
Returning to Visual Impressions in Graphic Design Archives and Creation as a Dialog

Throughout most of history, archives served first and foremost as places of order and narrative control, situated in an ecosystem defined by written language. In modern times, logistical frameworks in the shape of filing systems, standardized containers, and protective equipment has solidified them as a highly structured and nearly impenetrable space. This is reflected in the infrastructure of the graphic design archive, which prioritizes preservation over visual impressions, directing its access towards historians and researchers. However, graphic artifacts constitute a valuable source of knowledge for practitioners in the field, calling into question the text-based foundation underlying current archival practices. Drawing on firsthand experiences, this thesis explores the intersection of designer and archivist through visual systems. Contextualized within a web environment, the research draws on the interaction with digital tools as a central aspect of both contemporary archival and design practices. It investigates how manual interactions within a tool-based environment can shift the archive's function towards an involved visual dialog, thereby documenting internal processes onto both memory and image. The project positions the artifact itself as an interlocutor: a tool that triggers ideation and reintroduces the archive's epistemic structures into the creative process of practitioners.
